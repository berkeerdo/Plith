# Classic OSD and Settings Redesign Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Give the Classic OSD the same pass the notch got — its own icons, colours that carry meaning, a card sized by its fullest row, titles that scroll rather than ellipse — and rebuild Settings around what a person came to change.

**Architecture:** No new subsystems. Every change is either a view, a pure formatter with tests, or a token. `CardHost` keeps sole authority over card visibility; nothing here changes when the OSD appears or where it sits.

**Tech Stack:** WPF + .NET 10 (`net10.0-windows10.0.22000.0`), x64, xunit.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-10-osd-and-settings-redesign.md` — read it before Task 1, **including the correction block in the problem statement**. One of its three original claims was withdrawn after being checked against the code, and one turned out to need no new logic at all. Do not re-derive the problem from the mockup.

**Mockups:** `docs/superpowers/design/classic-osd.html` and `docs/superpowers/design/settings-design.html`.

## Task 1 — `AudioLabel`, pure

The two strings on the card that are currently built inline and cannot be tested.

- [ ] Write `tests/Plith.Tests/AudioLabelTests.cs` first. Cases for the short name: an adapter suffix is dropped (`Logitech G733 Gaming Headset (2- G733)` → `Logitech G733 Gaming Headset`); a single-adapter endpoint keeps its parenthesis meaningful (the case `ShortenFriendlyName` already protects); an empty or whitespace name falls back rather than returning empty; a name with no suffix is returned unchanged. Cases for the bus line: a Voicemeeter bus reads `Voicemeeter · A1`; a Windows endpoint reads `Windows · Output`; a muted rail reads `Muted` regardless of source.
- [ ] Implement `src/Plith/Services/AudioLabel.cs`. **Move** `ShortenFriendlyName` out of `WindowsAudioClient` into it rather than copying — a second copy is the thing that drifts.
- [ ] `WindowsAudioClient` calls it in **both** places: the endpoint enumeration (which already shortens) and `EmitSnapshot`'s `device.FriendlyName` (which does not). That single missing call is the whole of spec problem 3.
- [ ] Build, test, lint.
- [ ] Commit: `fix(audio): shorten the device name on the card, not only in settings`

## Task 6 — Settings: structure

The largest visual change, and the one with the least logic in it.

- [ ] A left rail with two groups — **Overlay** (Appearance, Audio, Media) and **App** (Theme, Shortcuts, Behaviour) — replacing the single scroll in config-file order.
- [ ] Notch-only rows stay **visible and indented under the presentation picker** rather than appearing and vanishing. Controls that disappear teach people the page is unpredictable; controls that are visibly subordinate teach them the rule. The fallback banner added in `e0e172e` moves with them.
- [ ] Every row's label and hint says what happens, not what the field is called. Where a setting has a threshold worth knowing — 85 %, 50 % — the hint says the number.
- [ ] No save button. The page already autosaves.
- [ ] Build, test, lint.
- [ ] Commit: `feat(settings): group the page by what people came to change`

## Task 7 — Settings: the live preview

- [ ] A preview pane showing the current presentation at rest, and reacting to the presentation picker and the resting-height slider **as they move**.
- [ ] It draws the shapes rather than hosting a real `OsdContent`: the OSD lives in an `HwndSource` in a z-band, and embedding it in a normal window is a different rendering path with different resource scope. A drawing that can be wrong is better than a host that can be a second, subtly different OSD.
- [ ] Build, test, lint.
- [ ] Commit: `feat(settings): show what a setting does instead of describing it`

## What this plan will not fix by being green

Everything in Tasks 2, 4, 6 and 7 renders in surfaces the suite cannot construct — the OSD is a layered window in a z-band, and the settings window is STA-only. The build, the tests and the lint together confirm that the code compiles, that the pure formatters are right, and that every interactive control has a name. They confirm nothing about what any of it looks like.

Two slices have now shipped green and then failed on the first real interaction. Plan for a session on real hardware after Task 4 and again after Task 7, and treat everything in §16 as open until then.
